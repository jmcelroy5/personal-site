Since moving to Colorado I discovered RAR Front Range, a wonderfully active chapter of [Radical Adventure Riders](https://radicaladventureriders.com/), a national organization dedicated to gender inclusivity and racial equity in cycling. 

RAR Front Range hosts several bikepacking overnighters and a very popular Gravel Camp every summer. I was lucky to join them on two overnight trips this year and had an incredible time. When they recently launched [a new website](https://rarfrontrange.com/) recently I volunteered to write up a trip report for the [blog](https://rarfrontrange.com/blog). I wanted to share it here, too:

---

Trip date: June 13–14, 2026 · [The Event](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/rar-spicy-bikepacking-overnighter-nederland-tickets-1987584679280) · [The Route](https://ridewithgps.com/routes/55336893)

It was mid-June, and I was riding away from the town of Nederland among a group of 10 fully loaded bikes, feeling excited but a bit nervous. This was my first trip with RAR Front Range, and it was also their first-ever "“spicy" level ride. My mind went through the usual paces for the first pedal strokes of the trip, anxiously cataloguing every item in my bike bags in an effort to identify what I'd forgotten this time.

I discovered RAR Front Range on Instagram in winter 2025 and couldn't wait for the next summer to join them on trips. I'd recently moved to Fort Collins, Colorado from out of state, where I'd had no trouble finding friends who bike and friends who camp but hadn't yet met anyone interested in combining the two. Now, I found myself riding in the biggest group I'd ever bike camped with! The distinct lack of dudes among us made the scene all the more surreal.

As we started climbing the road out of town I was worried about keeping up. But the pace stayed casual and social, especially once we got to a low-traffic gravel road and could ride side by side. As the day went on we organically split into smaller groups of similarly paced riders. The vibes were high with lots of chit chat, jokes, cheering, and snack sharing.

![Three loaded bikepackers riding side by side up a wide dirt road through aspen and pine forest]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/ned-spicy-bikepack-2026/image2.jpg){: .post-inline-image }

Pretty soon we were ripping down a steep road descent and crossing Boulder Canyon Road to Sugarloaf Road. It was as vertical as you'd expect Boulder-area riding to be, so we all geared down and settled into our own climbing rhythms.

A few of us stopped at a trailhead to regroup for a lunch break. We scarfed our PB&Js and PB&Jsedillas and admired each other's bikepacking setups. The next segment was a rowdy gravel descent down the Switzerland Trail. Some of our bags fell off with all the rattling, but between us we had enough straps to stabilize them again.

![A smiling rider on a loaded bike descending a rocky gravel road under pine trees, with another rider behind]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/ned-spicy-bikepack-2026/image3.jpg){: .post-inline-image }

We stopped at the bottom by a creek to fill up on water, since it was called out on the route map as our last refill opportunity for the day. While we rested in the shade and let our water filters do their thing, the group behind us caught up and joined in. We continued on together, our bikes much heavier with all the water, for the final push. On this stretch of the Swiss Trail the gravel was friendly, and the views were long.

Pretty soon a massive chimney rising up from an open field came into view, and that's how we knew we made it to the campground. Olly, our thoughtful and very fast ride leader, had arrived earlier and claimed a prime camping area for us.

With hours of daylight left, we spread out on the many picnic tables and talked languidly. We got an incredible amount of conversational mileage from a round of "“Marry, Bury, Fuck" with almonds, cashews, and peanuts. Who knew there were such strong feelings about nuts?

We considered riding a few extra miles to check out the Gold Hill store, but the clouds were turning dark, and we decided we should set up our tents. As soon as the rainflies were on, we started feeling drops. We hunkered down while the drops turned into a torrent and then died down again. A typical Colorado mountain storm and a good reminder of why RAR trips always start at 9:00am sharp.

![Colorful tents pitched in a grassy clearing ringed by pines at dusk, a camper in a puffy jacket standing among them]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/ned-spicy-bikepack-2026/image7.jpg){: .post-inline-image }

We'd lost our momentum for extra riding, but we were happy to hang out at camp and eat our hodgepodge of bagged dinners together. As the sun sank below the horizon we hung up the bear bags and decorated a nearby tree with our dangling camp mugs.

The next morning I woke to the sound of rain on the tent. It was super cozy to read my kindle and listen to the rain. But after an hour or so I was getting restless, and it didn't seem to be lightening up. Eventually I heard someone unzip their tent and yell out, "“We're in a cloud!" I poked my head out, and sure enough, it wasn't raining exactly, but we were encased in a fog so dense it was constantly spitting at us.

It was incredibly hard to leave the tent, knowing we'd have to pack up and bike out in that weather. That's when it occurred to me what I'd forgotten – a rain jacket and warm gloves! The forecast had lied to me, and I fell for it.

I finally enticed myself outside with the thought of a hot coffee. I packed up my tent first so there would be no going back, then joined the hardy group already making breakfast at the picnic tables. Some were sheltering with their stoves under the tables and inside the chimney.

With my first sip of coffee and the warmth of camaraderie my outlook improved measurably. It was shaping up to be a type 2 fun kind of day, but at least we were all in it together.

{% include carousel.html height="75" unit="%" number="2" %}

The ride began with a descent, and we had to stop a few times to rewarm our hands. I got the idea to use my wool socks as mittens and felt pretty dang clever. We warmed up on the next climb and started shedding layers. Spirits rose, and I was struck by the beauty of the gloomy sky and quiet dirt roads.

We reached an intersection where we had a choice to make. We could either turn right and do an extra gravel section that was supposed to be a highlight of the route, though we may have to ford a creek. Or we could turn left and zip down into town on a busy road. Everyone opted for the express route.

The descent was fast but painful with several regroups to warm our hands between our legs. Our leader and navigator, Jenny, identified a gravel side road we could take for a while to get off the road, and everyone was grateful for the slight detour.

We were all overjoyed when we reached the coffeeshop in Nederland. We cozied up around a table with hot drinks and pastries and let our frozen phalanges become a distant memory.

Jenny asked for our thoughts on RAR's first spicy ride, and we had a great convo reflecting on the weekend. The consensus was the first "“spicy" trip definitely delivered on spice! Some people felt underbiked at times with sub-40mm tires, but they made it through with extra street cred. Everyone felt the route was interesting, the distance was manageable, the company was stellar, and they had zero regrets.

![The riders smiling around a wooden table in a Nederland coffee shop with hot drinks]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/ned-spicy-bikepack-2026/image10.jpg){: .post-inline-image }

I felt like I'd known all the people around that table for more than just 24 hours. We exchanged contact info and talked about our bike touring plans and dreams. One woman, Cynthia, was preparing to do a ride across Colorado on the new Golden Gravel trail in a few weeks' time and invited everyone to join her at any point on her route. I felt so stoked to be plugged into a community of people doing cool things on bikes!

It's funny, if you'd told me ahead of time that I'd get so cold and wet I'd have to stop to get enough feeling in my fingers to pull the brakes, I probably would have stayed home. I'm *so* glad I didn't, though, because I would have missed out on the dreamy riding the first day, the shared challenge and triumph on the second day, and 9 new friends who share my weird, wonderful hobby. RAR Front Range's Nederland trip was a great success, and I couldn't wait for the next one!
